# Poem Summaries - New Rhyming Poems

## Poem 2 - "The Mechanism"
About moving through life on autopilot, when practiced routines become so automatic you no longer think about them. Your hand finds the rail, your feet know the route without fail. The morning builds itself from practiced, automatic parts—each motion on its shelf, each sequence learned by heart. You used to have to concentrate on every turn, but now muscle learns to navigate what mind need not discern. The mechanism runs beneath the level of thought. Sometimes you arrive at places with no memory of going—how you came to be alive here, now, without knowing. The autopilot hums. You're present but not quite. The body goes where habit knows, while you drift through the light.

## Poem 3 - "The Rehearsal"
About practicing and refining the self you present to the world. You practice being seen before the mirror, adjusting what you mean by speaking your name. The self you build each day is draft, not final thing—you try it on this way, then shift what you will bring. Not lying, just refining which version feels most true, the constant realigning of stranger into you. You watch yourself rehearse the gestures that convince, each movement you traverse in patterns learned long since. Some nights you forget which part was the disguise—the self you first project or the one behind your eyes. The rehearsal doesn't end when the mirror's done.

## Poem 6 - "The Offering"
About bringing your pain and burdens to an altar in a ritual that doesn't promise relief. Small things barely there, laid down with quiet care. Not prayer exactly, just the act of placing something down, a small and silent pact with what can't be found. You don't expect an answer or a voice to break the air—the ritual needs nothing grander than the simple fact you're there. Day after day you come to leave what weighs you most, the parts that make you numb, the fears you hold too close. Maybe nothing takes them, maybe they remain. But something shifts in bringing what you cannot name. The altar asks for nothing but your presence and your pain. Perhaps the offering is not to be received—perhaps it's the bringing that grants you some relief.

## Poem 9 - "The Choir"
About the multiple voices and selves that exist within one person. Sometimes you speak in unison—all voices aligned, one self, one settled mind. But other times they split apart, each singing their own line, a discord in the heart. One says stay, another go. One rages, one withdraws. They argue what you know and question every cause. You've tried to make them one again, to choose a single voice, but the choir won't abstain from making their own choice. Maybe you're not meant to sing with just one sound. Maybe what you've spent trying to quiet down is not a flaw to fix but the chorus of your truth—all these selves that mix and shift from root to root. Not fragmented, not undone. Just many voices learning how to become one.

## Poem 15 - "Threshold"
About standing in doorways and thresholds, caught between spaces. The physical experience of having one hand on the doorframe, weight balanced between two rooms you've crossed countless times before. It explores the condition of being neither here nor there—not entering, not leaving, just suspended in the liminal space between. About chronic indecision and the fear of commitment. The doorway holds what the rooms cannot—the possibility of staying undefined. The threshold becomes a place you refuse to lose because it's safer than committing to either side, caught between what is and what you miss.

## Poem 16 - "The Inheritance"
About discovering that you carry mannerisms, gestures, and physical patterns from people who are gone—likely parents or loved ones. These aren't consciously learned behaviors but unconsciously inherited ones that surface unexpectedly. You catch yourself making a gesture and realize you've seen it before in someone who's no longer here. The body remembers and continues what they began, making you an unwilling but faithful carrier of their presence.

## Poem 24 - "Shadow"
About watching shadows move across a wall throughout the day. The light begins sharp against the wall at dawn, thin and barely there. By afternoon the shadow has stretched and distorted, geometry re-sketched. As dusk falls, the edges blur and shadow crawls into the wall's veneer, no longer clear where shadow ends or if darkness blends. It's about the slow passage of time made visible through changing light and geometry, the patient observation of something so gradual you almost don't notice it changing. Time marked by the slow career of how a day can bend.

## Poem 26 - "Interval"
About tide pools and the brief stillness between waves. Water rushes in, fills the hollow in the rock, creates a moment of clarity where you can see the bottom and small life suspended there—then drains away as the next wave builds. It's about the rhythm you can't control, the cycle of filling and emptying, temporary containment followed by inevitable return to the ocean. The pool forms, holds briefly, then drains back. The cycle is as old as stone. About accepting what you cannot control and witnessing the brief window of stillness, knowing it's not permanent, just present.
