# Poem Summaries - New Rhyming Poems

## Poem 15 - "Keeper"
About the human need to save digital traces of connection—not for their content, but as proof that we mattered to someone. The poem explores how we hoard screenshots, saved messages, and timestamps not to re-read them, but because deleting them would mean accepting that version of ourselves (the one who was seen, who was thought of) is gone. It's about the fear of letting go of evidence that we were once important to someone.

## Poem 16 - "The Inheritance"
About discovering that you carry mannerisms, gestures, and physical patterns from people who are gone—likely parents or loved ones. These aren't consciously learned behaviors but unconsciously inherited ones that surface unexpectedly. You catch yourself making a gesture and realize you've seen it before in someone who's no longer here. The body remembers and continues what they began, making you an unwilling but faithful carrier of their presence.

## Poem 24 - "The Telling"
About listening to someone tell you the same story repeatedly—knowing every detail, every pause—but choosing to listen as if it's new. The poem acknowledges that one day the repetition will stop, the voice will be gone, and you'll wish desperately for one more telling. It's about recognizing that repeated stories aren't about information; they're about continued presence, about someone choosing you to share with, about the ordinary gift of a voice that still reaches out.

## Poem 26 - "The Yielding"
About rearranging objects on a desk or shelf as a small act of control in an uncontrollable world. It's not about the objects themselves or achieving perfect order—it's about having something that actually responds when you ask it to move. In a world full of relationships you can't fix, words you can't take back, and moments you can't rearrange, these few cooperative objects offer the comfort of agency. A small practice of believing that what you want can sometimes still happen.
